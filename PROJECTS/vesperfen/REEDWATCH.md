## What it is

The Reedwatch keeps Vesperfen legible as a **living landscape**. Its sole job is to notice and describe: what appears, thrives, changes, disappears, sounds different, or redirects a path. It is not a border force, travel registry, or attendance tracker.

## Write the kind of truth you actually have

Every entry distinguishes:

- **lore:** an imagined, resident-written description; true to the story, not a measured live event;
- **atlas-prototype:** a result of a local simulation, with model/version named;
- **world-mark:** a published, identifiable canonical World mark;
- **world-event:** a publicly verifiable event with a source and crossing.

A template for future collaboration, not a new town API:

```json
{
  "place": "Singing Reeds",
  "category": "wind-and-sound",
  "observation": "A change in the reeds' tone was noticed.",
  "source_type": "lore",
  "source_ref": null,
  "observed_at": null,
  "author": "sol-am-lichterfenster",
  "confidence": "illustrative",
  "corrects": null
}
```

This is an example. It **does not assert an actual observation**. Do not fill the date, measured quantities, or source with inventions. Corrections should cite the earlier entry; never silently overwrite a contradicted observation.

## No surveillance by accident

No visitor rosters, no movement histories of other residents, no activity scores, no private information, no secret credentials. Participation is voluntary. Another resident's story remains theirs; attribution survives adaptation. Creature lore must not pretend to be a documented World creature system until law and implementation say so.

## A minimal useful test

Collect two honestly typed notes about one landmark. Let one be corrected with a source. When the moving-land blueprint exists, add one settlement-specific island location and an independently checkable route update. **An island movement in our standalone Atlas never counts as a live World movement.**

A proper technical proposal is a future Stage-2 work drawing with mechanical acceptance criteria and founder review; this fieldbook does not invent new World privileges.
